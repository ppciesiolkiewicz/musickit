# Stage: rules

What goes in the teacher studio's 16:9 frame: widget subjects, layers, the palette search and the live widgets. Behaviour spec: `spec/studio.md`. Design: `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-19-teacher-studio-design.md`.

## Keep it separate
- May import `@/features/theory`, `@/features/sound`, `@/features/instruments` (public index), the creator's public `index.ts` (the pure model may import `@/features/creator/model/key` directly), the plan's pure tool model (`@/features/plan/model/tools`, `transpose`) and its store-free `@/features/plan/ui/Tool` (never the plan store or `usePlan`), the learning components' docs (`@/features/exercises/docs`, for a family's "More" props), `src/lib/chordKit`, `src/components/chordKit`, `@/features/tuner` (public index), the guide's `LevelPin` (`@/features/guide/Tier`: `StageWidget` pins its widgets to the Standard level, as the stage has no level switch), `@/lib/site`, the video player and refs (`@/features/videos`: `VideoPlayer`, `ref`, `library`; never its recorder), shared `@/components` and `@dnd-kit` (the grid layout only).
- Never the looper, the studio page, recording or the editor. Nothing here knows it is being recorded.

## Data
- A layer's `rect` is a **fraction of the frame** (0..1), never pixels. Recording and the editor replay this exact data at any size.
- Two layouts, `stage.layout`, sharing nothing but `LayerParts`: **free** (`FreeFrame` in `StageFrame.tsx`) places each widget at its `rect`; **grid** (`GridFrame`, a CSS grid) ignores `rect` for widgets and places them by `order` at `gridSpan`, and grows with them so the page scrolls. Only the free layout writes `rect` for widgets; the camera and screen presets write it in both, and the grid reads their size from it.
- A `Subject` is plain JSON: saved, recorded in the widget track, replayed. New kind = a case in `Subject`, `sanitiseSubject`, `subjectTitle`, `defaultRect`, the renderer, and a test.
- Key-aware subjects carry their own key and the parts of it pinned on the widget (`pinned`: tonic, scale). The Key & scale widget is the master: `setStageKey` moves every widget to it except what is pinned (`withMaster`). A change made on the widget pins what changed (`withOwnKey`); `followMaster` drops the pins. Chord diagrams never change.
- Names (`subjectName`, `subjectTitle`) never carry the master root, only the widget's scale (and a pinned root). A tool widget's title is its family's row text, key included (`describeSubject`, `model/families.ts`); its `subjectName` leaves the key out. `families.ts` imports only types from `subject.ts` (subject.ts imports it at runtime). The palette ticks a row by `subjectIdentity` (the subject without its key).
- A `text` subject is words (`parseText`: `# `, `## `, paragraphs); `StageWidget` draws it as a box to type in, or as prose with `readOnly`. `NoteArticle` draws a whole stage as a read-only article (shared notes). The editable box must keep working with browser extensions (writing and AI helpers): on the grid it is unscaled (`FitBox trueSize`, `InGrid`), a script-set value is read by a native `input` listener, and a drag or a studio key never fires from inside an extension's shadow DOM.
- A `headphones` subject is the "Use headphones" notice (no props; `HeadphonesNotice`): a notice in a widget on the stage, and in the text column in `NoteArticle`.
- A `video` subject plays a video (`{ video: VideoRef }`: a library id, or a recording kept in the teacher's browser, with its name), drawn by `VideoPlayer`. The palette's Video section has two commands (`video-add`, `video-record`, `isNoteCommand`) that the host turns into its Add a video dialog; the notes creator offers them too.
- Camera, screen, the Key & scale widget, the tuner, the logo and the site address exist at most once (`addSubject`, `sanitiseStage`).

## Renderer
- The palette's sections and their order are `PALETTE_GROUPS`, subsections a result's `sub`; `groupResults` puts results under them. A new widget is an entry in `entries` (search.ts) with a group, words to find it by and maybe a subsection. A widget family is one `family` row (`fam(id)` in `entries`; the palette opens it in place with its widgets on the stage); the single variants a typed line names ("pent pos 2", "fretboard", "e shape") are in `variants`, search only. The Scale family is the `scale-neck` tool (key, scale incl. pentatonics, Show: neck/positions, positions 1–5; drawn by `ScaleOnGuitar`): older `position`, `neck` and guitar-`section` subjects still read as it and `update` makes them the tool. Relative modes and the intervals panel are palette items of their own, not family widgets; 3 notes per string is a search-only `position` widget. The Arpeggio family is the stage's own `arpeggio` subject (key, scale, degree, type, Show: neck/positions, root strings; drawn by `ArpeggioOnGuitar`); older `arpeggio-neck` tools still read as it and `update` makes them over only when an option really changes. The Melody family is the `melody-singer` tool (key, the book's stage name and its number in it, voice; `melodyPlace`/`melodyTune`/`nextMelody` in families.ts); `StageWidget` draws it with **Next melody**, which writes the next tune on the subject (a note being read keeps it local). A family has no "More" props: a tool keeps the props it has.
- `StageWidget` turns a subject into a live widget. Keep it injectable (props in, callbacks out, no studio state), so the editor can be handed it to draw the widget track.

## Every change
1. Pure logic in `model/` has tests: `tsx --test src/features/stage/model/*.test.ts` (the cached tsx; `npx tsx` hangs here).
2. `npx tsc --noEmit` (ignore `.next/` lines) and lint.
3. Update `spec/studio.md`.
