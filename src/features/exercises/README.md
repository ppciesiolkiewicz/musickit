# Learning components

The views and the guided player the AI practice plan (`src/features/plan`) draws from. The plan only parses and
migrates; the components, their props and their docs live here.

## Layers

0. `timeline/` pure model: events, degrees, repeats, derived time. No React, no sound.
1. `shapes/` seven play-along shapes (`progression`, `two-scales`, `chord-steps`, `arpeggio-part`, `melody`, `solo-over`, `interval`);
   `expandShape` turns a shape into a timeline. A step is "strum", a degree, or a grip (`{ grip: ["1","3","5"], strings: "321" }`:
   notes struck together on chosen strings, a triad or a double stop), so `chord-steps` and `progression` can mix them into riffs.
   `interval` (`shapes/interval.ts`) is one interval, or a few in turn, as a shape on the neck, its two notes one after the other, one note a beat, in a major key: `strings` names the lower note's string and the upper note's ("64" two strings over, "65" the next string, "66" along one string), `direction` which note comes first (up the neck low note first, back down high note first, or both), `walk` whether it runs through the scale between two frets or goes above and below the key note. It is the tool's way from intervals into riffs and tunes: the same player, tab and neck as a `melody`. Every fret is chordKit's (`intervalPairs`, `intervalWalk`, `intervalFits` in `lib/chordKit/scaleChords.ts`, the code the double stops come from): the shape only puts the pairs in playing order, and each step names its string (`Step.string`), so `cuesOf` draws the note where chordKit put it. `intervalLine` says in words why a shape can't be played (strings that don't hold the interval within 4 frets, no place for the key note), and `intervalStep` reads the path step a shape stands for. `melody` takes `octave: 1` to play a tune an octave higher, refused where a note would pass fret 17.
2. `cues/` voicings, cues, tab and `chordCues`: what to show at each moment (`gripCells` puts a grip's notes on its strings, closed and within 4 frets, nearest the last grip); `visibility.ts` (each note hidden, muted,
   normal or highlighted, from two layers: `enable`, the notes in play, and `highlight`, those of interest) and `placement.ts` (which box, maybe an octave down, a `placement` asks for). Pure.
3. `play-along/` `PlayAlong`, the `guitar-play-along` tool (one clock, tab / notes / neck views), `schedule`, `solo`. Under single notes over bare
   chords it holds each chord (`withBackingSound`, the `under` prop: chords, drone, none). The neck view is the shared
   `ScaleNeck` with every place of the line at once (the playing chord's enabled notes normal, the highlighted ones
   ringed, the rest muted, the sounding ones lit); the notes view is the shared `SingingStaff`. Both label notes by the label setting and its "count from". `PlayerParts.tsx` is the player look shared with the backing track (card, round Play, tempo, beat meter, chord cards that keep one colour a chord), used by the play-along and `strum-along`.
4. `basics/<name>/` the basics: the theory tools on `key-panel` (`relative-modes`, `mode-intervals`, `key-chords`, `triads`, `progressions`, `circle-of-fifths`, `piano-keys`, `improvisation`, `tunings`, `chord-circle` with a `degree`, `caged-neck` with `shapes`: a theory page's panel in the tool's key and scale, from the creator's plugins and chordKit), `scale-neck` (its position boxes; with `parts` also the whole neck above them, the same marks on both, and chips to show or hide a part; with `chords`, numerals of the key, a chip per chord: pressing one rings its triad where the shown positions hold it on `chordStrings`, the rest faded, and strums it; the maths in `lib/chordKit/scaleChords.ts`), `choice` (a row of chips, not a tool: a section's choice, "Stage 1", "Variant 2" or plain values: in an exercise the step sets its other tools to the picked option's key, tempo, position and level), `chord-shapes`, `staff` (a "Start microphone" button; with `drone` / `click` its own backing with its own Play; with the mic its notes carry degree or solfège `labels`; its settings show as tags: key, voice, drone, click), `backing-band` (click, drone, drums and bass, each part offered `"on"`, `"off"` or `"always"`), `backing-track` (the backing tracks page's player, `BackingPlayer` from `@/features/backing`, set up from a curated progression or the lesson's own numerals, key, style, tempo and the parts that start muted, never saved; it is also the chords under a singer), `note-finder`,
   `interval-ears` (the ear game, `EarGame` of `components/practice` drawn `bare` from a settings query; its pure model is `lib/practice/earGame.ts`. Three kinds: `interval`, two notes named as an interval; `chord`, one chord named by its kind, the five default kinds or the `qualities` asked for (dim, aug, sus2, sus4, add9, 9, 13, dim7 too); `change`, a chord change: the I chord, then one other chord of a major key, both struck whole one after the other (`EarQuestion.chords`), named by the second chord's numeral, in a new key each question, levels IV and V, then vi, ii, iii, vii°), `arpeggio-neck`, `melody-singer`, `run-singer` (both with a chord a bar under the tune, worked out
   in `features/theory/melodyChords.ts`; a run announces each new key in its held-Do bar), `double-stops-neck` (the 3rds or 6ths of a key on one pair of strings, from one fret to another: the double stops page's neck, `OctaveNeck` and `useDyadPlayer` of `components/chordKit/DoubleStopsExplorer`, with no settings; each pair joined by a line, its chord named above and that chord's root ringed; a tap sounds a pair, Play runs the walk up and back down; the pairs are `dyadWalk` in `lib/chordKit/scaleChords.ts`, what the props may hold is `double-stops-neck/walk.ts`; no metronome), `song-chart` (a whole song's chords and form played by the backing band: each section of the form under its name, a box a bar, four to a row, a bar of two chords split in two, each chord with its numeral under it and one colour per chord; one bar of count-in clicks, then the bar playing is lit under its section and the line beside Play says the section, the bar and the time round; `Band`, `useBand` and `BandMixer` of `@/features/backing`, set up from the props and never saved, with `Band.setForm` for the count-in and for stopping after `repeat` times; `chart.ts` is the pure model: `layChart` the form in playing order, `chartNumerals` the same bars as the band's numerals, a chord over another bass note voiced as the chord itself (`chordNumeral`), and a chord the band cannot voice at all a rest of its length, so the bars keep their places, the chart marks the chord "band rests" and a notice under the chart names it, and `chartStep` the path step a chart stands for; with `hide` the chord names start hidden, every box showing its bar number, and Show chords / Hide chords switches them), `strum-along` (a metronome and the chords as boxes,
   one per bar or beat, each holding beat boxes of its strumming pattern, the playing strum lit; its maths in `blocks.ts`), `clap-along` (a one-bar rhythm to clap, tap or strum in time: `RhythmGame` of `@/features/rhythm` drawn `bare`, with the exercise's `pattern`, `bpm` and `bars` and no picker or setup panel; a count-in, notes moving into a line, each hit marked by how close to the beat it landed; Space or a tap, or the mic for a clap or a strum; the player can nudge the tempo and switch the input for that view, and the click, offset and mic sensitivity sit under its sliders button; Space goes through `useToolKeys`, so two on a page don't both fire), `staff-reading` (the game of naming a note on the staff: one note at a time drawn with "?" under it on the singing staff, `SingingStaff` with `unnamed`, no second renderer; answered by its letter button or letter key, or with the mic switched on by singing or playing it, `useMicPitch` and `micGate`, any octave; any answer moves on: the game says the note and where it sits, the note keeps its place with its name under it, sounds, and can be tapped to hear again, and the next is asked to its right, the last six on the staff; no score, no round; while the tool sounds a note the mic is not read, so the tool never answers itself; natural notes only; `clef` treble or bass and `notes`, the set asked; the pure game is `staff-reading/reading.ts`: the sets, `notesOf` from the staff's own `place`, `nextNote`, `answerLine`, `placeOf`), `string-singer` (the guitar's open strings sung along, at the guitar's own pitch: the strings, neighbouring pairs or the 5th-fret unisons; `strings.ts`), `interval-singer` (a named interval to sing from Do, "Interval · P5": Do sounds and sits on a piano roll, the default, or a staff; held on the other note it counts and shows; `play: "both"` sounds the note after Do, so the student copies the leap, the easier way; Do sits low enough in the voice that the leaps stay out of the top of it (`placeDo`); `dot: false` hides the voice, the harder way; `picker: false`, as an exercise draws it, leaves out the Keys switch and key chips; on a touch screen the mic is open only once the piano has finished, since a phone plays everything much quieter while the mic is open; while the voice holds the note its "?" fills (a bar on the roll, a ring on the staff; with the dot only); a Full screen button makes it a page modal (Esc closes it), the piano roll's rows grown to the screen's height; `asks.ts`, `IntervalRoll.tsx`). `breathwork` (breathing for singers, no mic: a circle that fills and empties with the breath and counts each phase, a strip of every phase, a tick per count and a chime per change on the audio clock, no spoken voice; drills `farinelli` (`from` to `to` counts), `sss-zzz`, `zzz-sss`, `cycles`, `long-exhale`, `body-scan` (`seconds`, `rounds`); the pure plan in `breaths.ts`, the sounds in `sound.ts`). `lip-roll` (a voice warm-up with the mic: a soft tone slides from head voice down to chest voice, up, as a siren or holds one chest-voice note while a playhead runs over its path and the voice's registers, and the mic draws the student's line over it in every pass, green where it is near; Tone (`play`): with the tone, the tone then a pass alone (its first note fading over the first flat), or no tone; Speed, its Accuracy and the Play-ahead (shared with the staff, `singing.lag`) are `LipRollSettings`: in a page's settings panel with `hostSettings`, under the tool's sliders button in a plan; each round a little deeper into chest voice; `glide.ts` the path and the round, `sound.ts` the tone). Where each voice type changes register (its two breaks, typical values) is `REGISTERS` in `@/features/theory/registers`; the hill's high place is head voice from it too. `voice-hill` (a voice warm-up with the mic, from the old attunr hill: one easy sound held low (a semitone lower each hold), in the middle or high in the voice while a ball on a slope or in a valley follows it; a ring round the ball fills while the voice stays on the green place, drains slowly off it and waits on a breath; `hill.ts`). `vowel-guide` (vowels on a held note, with the mic: a plain mouth drawn in code, no face, shows the vowel to sing, "ah", "eh", "ee", "oh" or "oo", and how its shape opens as the note climbs; the note plays, a ring round the mouth fills while the voice is on it, off the note it drains and a breath waits, as on the hill (`stepHold`); then the next vowel, and after the last the next note up, played in the breath; `climb` one-note, to-break or across-break; the mic reads the pitch only, never the vowel, and the tool says so; no score and no bell; the pure model is `vowel-guide/vowels.ts`: `targetNotes` from the hill's middle note and the voice's breaks, never nearer the top of the range than `UNDER_TOP`, `holdsOf`, `heardOf`, and the mouth as three numbers, `mouthShape`, `liftOf`, `blend`; how close counts is the singing accuracy, `useAccuracy`). The hill and the lip roll both read the voice with `voicePitch.ts` (`useVoicePitch`, `noteLabel`) and share `warmUpBar.tsx` (Start / Stop, Restart, Skip, the keys), and so does the vowel guide. Their runs live in refs apart from the props, so new props while running carry on with the new settings instead of stopping. The page `/theory/singing/warm-ups` (`WarmUpsApp`) offers both, their choices in its settings column from each tool's `settings.tsx` (`LipRollSettings`, `HillSettings`; `look` "bar" for a page's settings panel, "menu" for a tool's sliders button). `video` (a practice video of the library by its id, in `VideoPlayer` from `@/features/videos`, with the browser's own controls; the prompt lists only published videos). Each has `index.tsx` (the View) and `<name>.props.ts`.
   Shared: `levels.ts` (what a `level` stands for on each tool that takes one, as data: the plan's parser fills the settings in, the views never see a level; each tool's `level` prop says the same ladder in words, for the prompt), `choices.ts`, `arpeggio.ts`, `singer.tsx` (the two singers), `sing-along/` (their player: one green Play button that turns into Pause while the tune plays (Space presses it, so Space pauses and plays on), a Stop beside it (always shown, off until the tune plays), and the
   mic (plain, not green), mute buttons for the tune, its chords (when it has them) and a metronome on the tune's
   beats together in a frame under a speaker badge: the metronome is the singers' `metronome` prop, on by default for `string-singer`), a loop set by two markers on a strip of the tune, its passes laid end to end on an endless staff or a
   piano roll that keeps the voice's line, and a full screen page modal where the notes slide under a still bar; `passes.ts` is the pure model). Three more are guitar drills, each shown as tab with the fretting finger and pick stroke under every note, and a play button (`DrillPlayer.tsx`; the pure steps in `drills.ts`): `finger-pattern`, `picking-drill`, `legato-trill`.

`docs/` extracts each `@component` props file with the TypeScript compiler API into `docs.generated.json`
(`COMPONENT_DOCS`); a test fails when it is stale. Regenerate with `npm run docs:exercises`.

Settings come in three reaches. App-wide stores: the volume (`useVolume`, a master gain after the output bus, so the looper's tap stays at full level), the singing accuracy (`useAccuracy`) and the note labels (`useLabelSystem`). Per exercise: the melody view (staff or piano roll, the melody and run singers alike, switched inline on the sing-along player beside its mute buttons; `ExerciseSettings` in `src/components/practice/exerciseSettings.tsx`, wrapped round each plan exercise). Per tool: tempo, metronome, looping, as the tool's own state. `ToolSettings` (`@/features/settings`) shows them on a tool where there is no settings panel (the plan): the volume inline, the rest behind a sliders button (`Dropdown`), the same rows a page's `SettingsPanel` holds in their "menu" look; the staff, the sing-along player, the interval singer and the warm-ups have it. A tool on a page with a panel takes `hostSettings` and draws none of it: the page puts the rows in its panel. The play-ahead (`usePlayAhead`) is a saved store (`savedSetting`), read by the sing-along player itself and under its sliders button wherever it has no host panel, and the lip roll's accuracy (`useRollAccuracy`, values in `basics/rollAccuracy.ts`) is part of the player's records document (`useUserDoc("records")`, kept with the account), so a panel and its tool share them live. A staff written with `labels` leaves out the labels choice, since it doesn't follow the setting.

`src/components/practice/exerciseStep.tsx` is an exercise step (the plan's `Cells` wraps each section in one):
a tool hands it what it can start with `useStepPart` (the staff's mic and backing, `backing-track`, the play-along, the
singers, `PracticeBand`), and `StartExercise` ("Start exercise" / "Stop exercise") shows when the step has more than one kind
of thing to start. It starts the first part of each kind (one drone, not three) and plays them in one audio focus group
(`takeAudioFocus`'s `group`), so they don't stop each other; Stop stops every part, also those started by hand.
