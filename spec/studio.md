# Teacher studio

`/teachers/studio`. A recording studio for guitar lessons. Design: `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-19-teacher-studio-design.md`.

## Now
- **Teacher area:** `/teachers` links to the studio. A login will wrap it later.
- **Browsers:** any browser, at any width. Screen share needs a browser that has it, so on a phone it does nothing. On a window under 768 px the panel sits above the frame instead of beside it, folded unless it was unfolded before, and the frame takes the full width.
- **Layout:** the Add a widget button and the Layers list in a narrow left column; the 16:9 frame takes the rest (exactly what a recording will show), as wide as it can be without getting taller than the window.
- **Layers:** every widget in the frame: on Free the stack, newest first; on the Grid the page top to bottom, each row numbered, with **Move up** and **Move down** (what dragging does, for a keyboard or a program) in place of bring forward and send backward. Every row button is named with its widget (`Delete Text · Position 1`), a widget's frame on the Grid is a group named by its title, and a text box by its first line (`Text: Position 1`), so the page can be driven by its accessibility tree alone (a browser assistant). Each row selects its widget and has pin, hide/show, bring forward, send backward and delete. The chevron by **Add a widget** folds the panel to a narrow strip: unfold, add, and per widget a letter or two of its name (its full name on hover; click selects it, a hidden one is dimmed) and delete. The fold is remembered in `musickit.studio.panelFolded`. A **page** tag marks the bigger widgets (a whole cheatsheet page, Fretboard positions, Chords in the key) in the palette and the Layers list, as **control** marks Key & scale, the Metronome, the Tuner and the Audio input, and a rose **mic** tag the widgets the audio input lights notes on (fretboards, scale positions, chord boxes, sus & extension diagrams, Fretboard positions, Chords in the key, the compact CAGED panel, and the Scale, Modes, Chords, Progressions and CAGED pages; `takesMic`).
- **Palette:** `/` or ⌘K (or Add a widget) opens the widget picker over the page (never in the frame). Before anything is typed it lists every widget, by section with smaller subsection headings: **Key and sound** (Key & scale, Metronome, Tuner, Audio input), **Scale** (Scale, Relative modes, Intervals within each mode, Staff, Melody), **Chords** (Chord shapes; In the key: Chords in the key, Triads, Arpeggios, Arpeggio, Progressions; Sus & extensions: one circle diagram per chord), **CAGED** (CAGED; The whole neck: CAGED overview), **Theory** (Circle of fifths, Piano keys, Improvisation, Tunings), **Cheatsheet pages** (every cheatsheet page in full, as on `/cheatsheet`), **Video** (the **Video** and **Record a video** commands, which open Add a video), **Brand** (Logo, Site address) and **Studio** (camera and screen-share commands). Controls (Key & scale, Metronome, Tuner, Audio input: they set, play or listen to something rather than show it) carry a small amber `control` badge, here and in the Layers list. A **widget family** (see below) is one row, with the key it will be in on the right and, when the stage has widgets of it, a small green count. Everything happens inside the list: there is no second page or dialog. Clicking a family row (or Enter on it) opens it **in place**, one family at a time (clicking it again closes it): its options (a pick of one as chips, or a select when it has many; toggles for boxes, positions or shapes with an **All** toggle, CAGED's Overview standing alone, an arpeggio keeping its two last boxes; typed text for a chord or chords). There is no "More" fold: a widget keeps the other props it has. They start from what the family makes in the row's key (box 1, the key's own scale and chord). **Add to stage**, Enter in the search while a family is open (on its row or on any row that is not another family), Enter on an option or Enter in a field adds one widget, pinned where its key differs from the master (one made on a stage with no root is made in C and not pinned, so it moves to the root once one is set); Space toggles an option. The family stays open, and below the button it lists its widgets on the stage, each by its row text (`Scale · C major pentatonic · positions 1–2`), so a widget sits with the others like it: the row's pencil opens the same options to edit it in place, changing the widget at once (typed text on Enter, on leaving the field, or when its options close: Esc, collapsing, closing the picker; a cleared or mistyped chord keeps the chord it had; its last box can't be unticked), and ✕ takes it off. Esc closes an open family first, then the picker; a click outside closes it. Any other row is ticked when its widget is on the stage, with a green outline: click it to put it on the stage or take it off; Enter in the search always adds one (a second one when it is there already); the picker stays open, so several can be ticked at once. A command runs and closes it. ↑ ↓ move between rows. Typing closes an open family and searches names and other words (`fretboard`, `whole neck`, `same notes`, `intervals`, `metronome`, `drone`, `tuner`, `triads page`, `e shape`, `a minor c shape`, `sus iv`, `c major 3nps box 2`, `camera corner`); a key (`a dorian`) or a mode alone (`ionian`, `dorian`: that mode on the master root) lists the widget families, one row each in that key (Scale first), never a command, and opens the first. A typed box, position, view or shape still adds that one widget with one Enter: `c major 3nps box 2` is the second 3 notes per string position, `fretboard` the neck alone (`overview`: neck and positions), `same notes` Relative modes, `intervals` Intervals within each mode, `e shape` a CAGED widget with the E shape (`c shape a shape` stacks two). There are no pentatonic box or scale position rows: pentatonics are scales in the Scale family's dropdown. A typed key is pinned on it (a tool widget pins its tonic only). On a tie, a single widget ranks above a family row. Search keeps its best result first and orders the sections by their best result. There is no single-chord widget (the Chords page has a chord search), and no compact Chords or Modes panel (Chords in the key and Relative modes show the same, with chord shapes).
- **Widgets:** names say what the widget is and its scale, never the master root (`Fretboard · Dorian`, `CAGED E shape (root on low E) · minor`); a root pinned on the widget shows (`Fretboard · G Dorian`). The **Key & scale** widget is the master: the root (or none), the scale family, the mode and the note labels. Every widget added takes the master key and scale and follows it. On hover, each key-aware widget shows two tags in its top-left corner, its root and its scale (`data-chrome`); changing one pins that part on the widget, so the master no longer moves it: a widget pinned to Dorian keeps Dorian when the master scale changes, and still moves to a new master root, so A Dorian and A Phrygian side by side both become C Dorian and C Phrygian. A key picked inside a widget (a mode in Relative modes) pins the same way; a key typed in the search is pinned where it differs from the master. A pinned widget's tags have **Follow** to go back to the master. The Metronome follows the root only. CAGED widgets use the minor shapes when their key's third is minor. A CAGED widget can hold several shapes (`shapes`, in CAGED order C A G E D, stacked in a column, named `CAGED shapes A, E`); `shape` alone is one box, as before, and one letter picked in the CAGED family is saved that way. A whole cheatsheet page, and Chords in the key, fit the widget's width and scroll inside it. Every widget is an opaque dark card, so a widget over another hides it (the logo and site address stay see-through over the picture). On hover every widget shows a small toolbar (`data-chrome`) just outside its top-right corner, so it never covers the widget: above it, or below it when the widget touches the frame's top, inside the corner only when it fills the frame's height (and always for the brand marks). It has pin, delete (camera and screen share keep their own close) and **Fill the frame**: the widget, brought to the top, as big as the frame allows in its own shape (the whole frame when shapes are not kept), centred; the same button (**Back to its place**) returns it. Moving or resizing a filling widget keeps it where it is put. Brand marks have no fill button. The Tuner is the `/tuner` tuner on the studio's audio input: no microphone or input picker of its own, and its Start / Stop turns the audio input on and off. **Pin** (in the hover toolbar, lit while pinned; also in each Layers row) keeps a widget over every widget that is not pinned, whatever is raised or selected, and is saved with the stage. The studio has **one audio input**, and it feeds every widget. The **Audio input** widget (at most once; saved as kind `mic`) shows it. It has two sources, as in the looper: **Guitar** (by default Input 1 of the device) and **Voice** (by default both channels). A switch on the widget picks which one listens. The cog opens a dialog: each source's device (or the site's input from the devices menu) and channel (Input 1, Input 2, Both), **Use this** to switch, and the output list. The setup is kept in `musickit.studio.audioInput`. Picking the widget in the palette starts listening, and Listen / Stop toggles it; a stage opened with one waits for Listen. The widget shows the note heard and how far off it is, with the tuner's compact meter (needle, Hz, ± cents). The input belongs to the studio, not the widget: hiding the widget keeps it listening; it stops when neither it nor the Tuner is left. The heard note lights up (a blue ring, on every place that note falls) on the pinned widgets and the selected one: fretboards, positions and chord boxes, including inside cheatsheet panels. Brand widgets: the attunr logo and the site address, each on a dark pill, each at most once, in fixed places in the top-right corner (the address under the logo): they cannot be moved, resized or zoomed, only shown or hidden (ticked in the palette, the eye in Layers, or the ✕); a stage saved with one elsewhere puts it back; the Key & scale widget and the Tuner exist at most once too. A stage saved with the old single Sus and extensions section opens with the first chord's diagram; a saved chord box still shows.
- **Layouts:** in the video studio, the selector above the frame's right corner (never recorded) has **Grid** and **Free**, then **Auto layout** on Free (the studio's Grid has no button). The notes creator has no selector: it is always the Grid with One per row on. The choice is kept in the stage (`layout`, saved and shared in notes). A new, empty stage is on the Grid; a stage saved before layouts existed stays Free, as it was arranged. Neither layout changes the other's arrangement.
  - **Grid:** a CSS grid of 12 columns. Each widget spans a quarter, a third, a half or all of it, from its kind (`gridSpan`), and takes the shape of its content, so it has no resize dots or fill button. Its width can be set by hand instead: on hover or selection a thin bar on its right edge drags it wider or narrower (15% of the frame up to all of it, in whole percent; the widget stays centred, so it grows on both sides, and its height follows its shape), and a double click on the bar gives back its kind's width. The width is kept on the layer (`gridWidth`, a share of the frame's width; saved and shared in notes). With One per row off it takes the fewest of those spans that hold it, centred in them. A page has no bar. A **page** widget (a whole cheatsheet page, Fretboard positions, Chords in the key) takes the frame's whole width and one screen's height, and scrolls inside itself; a strip stays free on its right, where the wheel or a finger still scrolls the page. It is laid out at a page's width and fits the box, never cut off. Dragging a widget changes the order (`order`, `@dnd-kit`), not the Layers list; a drag never starts on a widget's own controls. An arrow key moves the selected widget one place; ⌥← and ⌥→ make it narrower or wider, and ⌥↑ and ⌥↓ do nothing. A new widget always goes at the end and is scrolled into view. The frame is at least 16:9 and grows with its widgets, and the page's own scroll goes through them; the selector and the side panel stay in view. The camera and screen share are grid items sized by their presets; the brand marks keep their corner. **On a frame under 640 px** (a phone) a quarter or third widget takes half a row and anything bigger the whole row, a page is 80% of the screen's height and reflows at the frame's width instead of shrinking, and a drag starts after holding a finger on the widget for a moment, so a swipe scrolls.
  - **One per row** (the notes creator's Grid) is kept in the stage (`gridAuto`, saved and shared in notes). The notes creator puts every stage it opens or loads on the Grid with it on (`notesStart`): its saved stage, a shared note, a preset, the JSON applied; a note made on Free opens in grid order. There is no switch. On, most widgets get a row of their own, centred (`gridAutoPlace`): a widget with a neck (`hasNeck`: scale and position necks, CAGED, arpeggio, the neck tools) takes 90% of the width, anything else its usual width. A page keeps its whole row, and a quarter-wide widget (a chord) still shares its row. A width set by hand wins over these: the widget keeps its own row at that width (a chord stays in a shared row, in the fewest spans that hold it). Off, the Grid is as described above. A phone-wide frame keeps its usual widths and only stacks by the narrow spans.
  - **Free:** the rest of this section. **Auto layout** (once per press) puts the widgets in rows inside the frame, in reading order, each keeping its shape and all scaled down together when they would not fit. The camera and screen share stay where they are.
- **Moving:** drag a widget (from its top strip at once, from anywhere after a few pixels, so clicks still play), resize from any of its four corner dots (the opposite corner stays put). Selecting a widget (a click, a drag, its Layers row) brings it to the top (camera and screen share keep their place under the widgets). Stacking is each layer's own `z`, apart from the Layers list: the list keeps the order widgets were added (newest first), and bringing forward or sending backward changes only the stacking. A stage saved before `z` stacks in list order. The outline and dots show only on hover or when selected. **Keep widget shapes** (a switch under the Layers list, on by default; its tooltip explains it): each widget's frame takes the shape of what it shows, so there is no empty margin around it (moved up or left just enough when its new shape would hang out of the frame), and a corner drag keeps that shape (whichever way the corner moved more sets the size). Off, a frame can be any shape and the widget sits centred in it. The choice is remembered in `musickit.studio.keepShape`. A widget whose content is wider than its design width is scaled down in whole, never cut off.
- **Zoom:** hold ⌘ and scroll, or pinch on the trackpad, to zoom into a widget's content around the pointer (up to 6×); a plain scroll pans a zoomed widget; `0` resets the selected one. The widget's box stays the same size; the zoom is saved with the layer.
- **Add a widget:** the button at the top left (`/` or ⌘K) opens the palette. **Keys** (all of them with `?`): `/` or ⌘K add, `C` camera (minimized), `F` fullscreen or minimize the camera, ⇧F the same for the screen share, arrows move (Shift: more), ⌥+arrows resize, `0` reset zoom, ⌫ delete, Esc deselect. None fire while typing in a field.
- **Camera:** a layer like any other: drag, resize, reorder, hide or delete it. The palette offers whichever applies: **Camera** (adds it, minimized), **Fullscreen camera** / **Minimize camera** (whichever it is not now; minimized = the bottom-right portrait corner) and **Close camera**. Hovering the camera shows **Mirror camera** and the same fullscreen/minimize and close buttons in its top-right corner (never recorded). Mirror camera flips the picture left to right, as in a mirror, in the recording too; it is lit while on, off by default, and remembered in `musickit.studio.cameraMirror`. It never opens on its own: the layer shows "Start camera" until clicked (also after a reload). Refused or unplugged, it says "Camera off: click to retry". With more than one camera, a picker shows under the Layers list. Video only; the camera's choice is remembered in `musickit.studio.camera`.
- **Screen share:** **Screen share** asks Chrome for a window or screen and adds it as a layer at the bottom, full frame, shown whole (letterboxed). Then the palette offers **Fullscreen screen share** / **Minimize screen share** (minimized = bottom left) and **Close screen share**, and hovering it shows the same corner buttons. No sound. Stopping it from Chrome's bar or closing or deleting the layer ends it. After a reload the layer asks to share again.
- **Video widgets:** more subject kinds, `melody` (optional `beats` across, 2 to 16), `countdown`, `intro`, `progress` and `next`, exist for widget videos (`videos/*.json`), not for the studio: the palette never offers them. They follow a host timeline (`TimelineContext`) and draw nothing without one. The melody's following view rides the bar steadily and jumps back on the wrap (`steadyCamera` on `MelodyStaff`, off everywhere else).
- **Tool widgets:** a subject kind `tool` holds one of the AI plan's learning components (`scale-neck`, `position-neck`, `arpeggio-neck`, `chord-shapes`, `staff`, `melody-singer`, `run-singer`, `backing-track`; `STAGE_TOOLS`), stored as the plan parses it; any other component, or one that doesn't parse, is dropped on load. The master moves its key (an arpeggio's chord root) and never its scale; a key changed on the widget pins the tonic, the only part a tool widget can pin. Chord shapes have no key and never move. It is drawn by the plan's own component in a dark card, scaled into its rect like any widget, always with every control: the stage pins the guide's level to Standard, whatever level is set on the site's pages. On hover it shows its root tag only (none for chord shapes): changing it pins the tonic, and there is no scale tag, as the scale is the widget's own. A staff (its mic is on unless the plan turned it off) listens to the studio's audio input: no microphone or input chip of its own, its Start / Stop turns the audio input on and off, and it carries the mic badge. Where there is no studio audio input (the notes creator, the editor replay) it has no microphone at all. Scale necks, arpeggio necks and chord shapes ring the heard note and carry the badge too; the position neck does not. Offered in the palette through the widget families.
- **Widget families** (`stage/model/families.ts`, curated): **Scale**, **CAGED** (the `caged` subject: Overview or any of C A G E D), **Arpeggio** (the stage's `arpeggio` subject, drawn as the arpeggios page's neck and positions by `ArpeggioOnGuitar`: a **Key** select, a **Scale** select (every mode), **On** (the key's chords, named in the key and scale picked), **Type** (the scale's triad, 7th or 9th, or any quality), **Show** (neck, positions) and **Root on** (6th to 2nd string, with positions); it follows the master, and a key or scale picked here pins that part; an older `arpeggio-neck` widget still loads and draws as it was, and is made over into this one, on the nearest type, only when an option really changes), **Chord shapes** (chords, open or barre), **Staff** (a scale, a voice), **Melody** (the `melody-singer` tool: a tune from the singing page's melody book, picked by **Key**, **Melody** (the book's stage name, `Do Re Mi`) and **Number** (its place in that stage, from 1), and **Voice**; named `Melody · D major · Do Re Mi 3, alto`). A Melody widget has **Next melody →** over it: the next tune in the book (back to the first after the last), saved on the widget, so a shared note opens on it; in a note being read it changes only what that reader sees. Picking a new melody name in the family starts at its first tune. Scale is the `scale-neck` tool drawn as the scales page's guitar section (`ScaleOnGuitar`, so tapping a position's name lights it up on the neck): a **Key** select, a **Scale** select (every mode and every pentatonic, the blues scales too), **Show** toggles Neck and Positions (not radios; the last can't be unticked on a widget) and, only while Positions is on, positions 1–5 with All. The tool's `neck` / `positions: false` props carry Show (absent: positions only, as in the plan). Picking a key on a widget pins its tonic; a tool's scale is always its own. Older `neck`, guitar `section` and single Pentatonic box / Scale position subjects load unchanged, read as Scale and become the tool when edited. Intervals within each mode (the compact `scale` panel), Relative modes (`section` `relatives`) and 3 notes per string (`position`, search only) are widgets of their own, not family widgets. Each family's main props start at the key's own scale (all five positions) and the key's own chord (I IV V for chord shapes). A widget of a family is named by one line: the family, its key, its main props (`Scale · C major pentatonic · positions 1–2`, `Scale · D Dorian · neck, positions 1–5`, `CAGED · C major · E, A shapes`); a tool widget's title is that line, its key included, as its key and scale are its own props; the other widgets keep their own titles (`3 notes per string 2 · Dorian`, `Intervals within each mode · Dorian`, `Relative modes · Dorian`). The palette lists each family once (`family` results, `search.ts`); its single variants are found by typing only.
- **Video preview:** **Preview a video…** under the Layers list (a play button in the folded panel) picks a video config (`videos/*.json`, Chrome's file picker) and plays it in place of the stage: the frame the render draws (`spec/video.md`), scaled to fit, with play/pause, a slider to any moment, a mark per tune to jump to its count-in, the time, the frame number and where it is ("Tune 2 · pass 3 of 4"), and 16:9 / 9:16 when the config has both. Space plays or pauses, ← → step a frame (⇧: a second); the stage's own keys are off meanwhile. It plays the render's soundtrack (the piano and the click, rendered in the page for each version of the file, "preparing the sound…" until then), the picture following the sound's clock; the speaker button mutes it. The file is checked every second: a save reloads it, and a save that does not parse shows its error over the last good frame. ✕ goes back to the stage. Not remembered across reloads.
- **Saved:** the stage (layers, their place and size as fractions of the frame, the Key widget's key) is saved in `musickit.studio.stage` and comes back on the next visit.

## Notes creator

`/teachers/studio?notes=1` (the Theory menu's **Notes creator**, which replaced the Cheatsheet builder; `/cheatsheet`, `/creator` and `/theory/cheatsheet` redirect there). It opens in the site's page frame, like the other tools: the nav, the title with a line about the page, and the footer, with the stage filling the window's width under them. The same stage and palette (the same widget families, each opening in place with its widgets on the stage) with no video preview, camera, screen share, Audio input or Tuner, and it runs in any browser wide enough to lay out (no recording, so no element-capture check). Its stage is saved apart in `musickit.studio.notes`. Over the page, three numbered steps (`NOTE_STEPS`): press Add a widget to put a scale, a chord box, a neck, a video or some text on the page; drag a widget to move it; press View as a note to see the page as students will. There is no pinning (no Pin in the hover toolbar or the Layers rows; `pin={false}` on `StageFrame`). **View as a note** (under Share as a note, and an eye in the folded strip) shows the note as a demo note's page draws it, in place of the panel and the stage: the page's header becomes the note's own (**A note from your teacher** over the note's `# ` title and the line after it; only the eyebrow when the note opens with something else), and under it the article, read-only, the header and the article in one centred column as wide as a note's own page (`NOTE_COLUMN`), with the voice picker when it sings and without Make your own note. **← Back to editing**, a quiet link-like button with a left arrow over the eyebrow at the top of the page, returns to the creator; the stage's keys are off meanwhile. The header follows through `NoteViewProvider` and `NotesCreatorHeader`, around the page frame on `/teachers/studio?notes=1` and `/note/<slug>?edit=true`. **Share as a note is a demo** (in the notes creator and the video studio; the folded strip's share button unfolds the panel and opens it): pressing it opens an amber popover beside the button (`SharePopover`, drawn on the page so the panel never clips it; under the button on a narrow window), "Sharing isn't open yet" and "This is a demo, so a note can't be sent as a link yet.", in the notes creator with "Press View as a note to see the note as your students will." and a **View as a note** button. ✕, Esc or a press outside closes it. Nothing is copied. Links made before still open as notes. When sharing opens, it copies `…?notes=1#note=<z|j><base64url>`: the layers and key as JSON, deflated when the browser can (`z`) or plain (`j`); live widgets are dropped. The video studio has the same **Share as a note** button (beside the video preview one, in the folded strip too): it shares the stage as a note and opens in the notes creator. Opening such a link shows the note as an **article** to read, not the creator (`NoteView`, `NoteArticle`): one column the width of the page in the grid's order, text as headings and paragraphs in a narrower centred column, each widget as wide as it was set by hand on the grid, else as wide as its kind is there (never under 40% of the column; a neck or position box the whole column) in the shape of its own content, a page grows to its content (nothing scrolls inside, no scrollbars; `FlowInBox`), spaced in sections: a text that opens with a heading stands 3rem (3.5rem from tablet width) clear of what is over it, a widget sits 1.25rem under the words about it, anything else 1.5rem apart; inside a text, a heading further down stands clear of the paragraph over it. **Make your own note** sits under a rule, 3rem below the article. `/note/<slug>` pages use the page frame's `tight` side margins (5rem each side on desktop, 6rem on wide screens). Nothing to edit: no palette, layers, layout selector, key tags, drag or share. Widgets still play, but change nothing. The creator's own saved stage (`musickit.studio.notes`) is left alone, and **Share as a note** no longer rewrites the address bar, so a reload keeps editing. A link that does not decode says so, with **Make your own note**. Code: `notes.ts`, `NoteView.tsx`, `stage/render/NoteArticle.tsx`.

**Text widget:** subject kind `text` (`{ text }`, up to 4000 characters), in the palette's **Text** section (many allowed, never ticked). On the stage it is a box to type in; in a note it reads as prose: `# ` starts a title (drawn like the page title), `## ` a heading, `### ` a subheading, a blank line a new paragraph (`parseText`). It takes a whole grid row. Empty text is left out of an article. The box works with the browser's and extensions' writing and AI helpers (Grammarly, LanguageTool, an AI assistant that rewrites the field): on the Grid it is drawn at its true size, not scaled, so their marks and popups sit on the words; a value an extension sets from a script is kept like typing; a press in an extension's popup never starts a drag, and the studio's keys stay off while its own box has the cursor.

**Use headphones notice:** subject kind `headphones` (no props, fixed words: `HeadphonesNotice`), in the palette's **Text** section as **Use headphones**, for a note that listens with the microphone while it plays. An amber notice with the headphones icon; in a note it sits in the text column, not in a widget frame. It takes a whole grid row. The `minor-pentatonic` note has one over its first melody.

**Video widget:** subject kind `video` (`{ video: { source: "library", id } | { source: "recording", id, title } | { source: "placeholder" } }`; `@/features/videos`), half a grid row, played with the browser's own controls. The palette's **Video** section has **Video** (opens **Add a video** on **My videos**) and **Record a video** (opens it on **Record**); the dialog sits over the page and the stage's keys are off while it is open. Its tabs, in order: **My videos** (the teacher's recordings, newest first: **Watch**, **Add to the note**, **Download**, **Delete** with a confirm that says a note holding it can no longer play it; empty, it offers **Record a video**), **Record** (**Start camera** asks for the camera and the microphone, never before the click; the live picture is mirrored; **Record** / **Stop** with a clock, a take stops itself at 10:00; then the take to watch, a **Name**, a date by default, **Save and add to the note**, **Record again** and **Download**), **Placeholder** (**Add a placeholder**: a place for a video to make later) and, last, **attunr video library** (the published practice videos, horizontal 16:9 only, `library.ts`: name, area, length and a line, **Watch** and **Add to the note**). A placeholder in the creator is a dashed card, **Record this video**: pressing it opens Add a video on **Record**, and the video saved or picked there (any tab) takes the placeholder's place; in a note being read it is a card that says **Video coming soon** (`VideoPickContext` is absent there). A saved recording is kept in this browser (IndexedDB `musickit-videos`) and nowhere else: a note holding it plays it only in the browser that recorded it; elsewhere the widget says so, by its name. The video studio has the same commands, adding to the stage. In an article a video is a widget like any other.

**Edit as JSON (dev)** (notes creator, development only, `devUi()`; under View as a note) opens the note as text in one box, **Note JSON**: `{ "key": …, "widgets": [ … ] }`, the widgets' subjects in page order, with no ids, places or sizes (`noteText`). **Apply** replaces the whole note with what the text says (`readNoteText`, sanitised like a saved stage, on the Grid with One per row on); a widget that cannot be read is left out and named by its place and kind in the dialog's status line, which stays open then; text that is not JSON, or has no `widgets` list, changes nothing and says why. Esc, Cancel or a click outside closes it. Code: `NoteJson.tsx`, `notes.ts`.

## Preset notes
`/note/<slug>` (e.g. `/note/minor-pentatonic`) opens a note made ahead, read as an article like a shared note. Its stage is in code (`presetNotes.ts`), its title and line in `presetInfo.ts` (the server page reads only that); the page is `PresetNote`, with **A note from your teacher** over its title (the page frame's `eyebrow`) and the audio devices button in the nav, as its tunes listen. The note opens with its own `# ` title and, straight after it, the line about the note (so both can be changed in the creator), kept equal to `presetInfo.ts`'s by a test; the page's header draws them as any page's title and intro, with the short rule (`PresetHeader`, `noteHeader`; `PageHeading`), and the article leaves them out. A heading and its words come before the widget they are about. `minor-pentatonic` is position 1, the whole neck, the key's chords and a rock backing track to play over (`backing-track`: the Aeolian vamp Am F G Am, no drone), then two melodies in C (Do Re Mi, then up to Sol and La: the same five notes) and a pentatonic run to sing; a run (`run-singer`) and a backing track have no palette family, so only a preset holds one. `three-chords-in-g` is the G, C and D chord boxes, the chords of G major, then one backing track, the three-chord rock groove (G G C D) at 70 bpm; a strum-along is not a stage tool (`STAGE_TOOLS`), so a note can't hold one. In an article a melody, a run or a backing track is as wide as the column, like a neck. `/note/<slug>?edit=true` opens the same note in the notes creator, to change it (`StudioApp`'s `preset`); **Edit this note** in the title row's right corner (the page frame's `action`) goes there, and **View the note** there comes back: it starts as the preset on every visit and reads and saves nothing, so the creator's own saved stage (`musickit.studio.notes`) is left alone and a reload brings the note back; as a dev demo every change is kept in the tab's memory as it is made (`savePresetDraft`, `presetStage`; there is no Save button), so **View the note** and coming back to edit show the changes, until a reload; the note page's header shows the changed title and line too; **Share as a note** works as usual, and its link is always the notes creator's own address (`/teachers/studio?notes=1#note=…`). The page is not indexed. Tests check every preset survives the note sanitiser, and that the tunes its text describes are still at their numbers in the singing books (`PRESET_TUNES`).
