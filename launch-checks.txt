======================== How this list works ========================
Legal and privacy things to check again before each kind of launch. tasks.txt is for what to build; this is for what
to re-check, because the law or a vendor may have changed since the last look.
- One section per launch. Before that launch, go through its section top to bottom.
- Each check: `<Series><n>:` what to check, then why, where it lives in the code or on the page, and the last result.
- A check is never deleted. After checking, add a dated note under it, newest last: `[<date> checked] what was found,
  what was changed`. A check that needed work also gets a task in tasks.txt, named here.
- The law notes here came mostly from law-firm and vendor summaries, not from a lawyer. Before paid plans open, a
  lawyer reads /terms and /privacy.
- Where the promises live: /terms (src/app/terms/page.tsx), /privacy (src/app/privacy/page.tsx), the counting rules
  (src/lib/analyticsChoice.ts), PostHog (instrumentation-client.ts). A change to what the app does with data is a change
  to /privacy and a new PRIVACY_UPDATED (src/features/account/terms.ts).

======================== First news or offers email ========================
Email1: Every news email has a working unsubscribe link and says who sent it (attunr, the ABN, hello@attunr.app).
  Spam Act 2003: the link must work for 30 days and be honoured within 5 working days. Nothing in the mail code does
  this yet (src/features/account/server/mail.ts sends log-in mail only).

Email2: Only accounts with users.email_updates_at set get it. That column is the proof of consent: keep it.

Email3: A list of addresses that said no, checked before every send, kept even after an account is deleted.

Email4: Sending to the US adds a postal address in the email (CAN-SPAM). Sending to the EU or UK: opt-in is already
  the rule here.

======================== Marketing to the EU or the UK ========================
Merely being reachable from Europe is not this launch. Prices in euros or pounds, another language, or adverts aimed
there are.
EU1: A data processing agreement is in place with each company in Every1. Most publish a standard one: some apply
  through their terms, some need a click or a form in the dashboard. Note here which is which, with the date.

EU2: /privacy says what makes sending data out of the EU lawful, per company: the EU-US Data Privacy Framework
  (check the company is certified) or the EU's standard contract clauses, from that same agreement.
  The Framework was upheld in September 2025; an appeal was pending when last checked.

EU3: Does cookieless counting need consent? Today the app asks first in the EU, EEA and Switzerland and counts until
  told no in the UK (the UK's 2025 Act allows statistics with a way to say no). The EU's "Digital Omnibus" may relax
  the EU rule: if it becomes law, the ask-first list in analyticsChoice.ts can shrink.

EU4: An EU representative and a UK one (GDPR Article 27): arguably needed once Europe is a target market.

EU5: A simple record of what data is processed, why, and by whom (GDPR Article 30). /privacy is most of it.

EU6: A yes to counting is asked again after 6 to 12 months. Not built: the answer is kept with no date.

EU7: A copy of your data as a file, from /account. Not built: /privacy says to email us, answered within 30 days.

EU8: The UK Children's Code applies to a service UK children are likely to use: high-privacy defaults, no profiling,
  no marketing to under-18s. Today: no adverts, news emails off by default.


======================== Marketing to the United States ========================
US1: A child under 13 (COPPA). A teacher adding a student under 13 needs the parent's consent, and a private teacher
  may not count as a "school" that can consent for them. The app neither asks an age nor checks consent: /terms puts
  that on the teacher. Decide: ask the teacher to confirm, or keep under-13s out.

US2: California privacy law (CCPA) starts at USD 26.6 million revenue or 100,000 Californian users. Far off; the app
  already honours Global Privacy Control.

US3: The FTC's "click to cancel" rule was struck down in July 2025 and a new one was being written. Re-check with
  Paid3.
